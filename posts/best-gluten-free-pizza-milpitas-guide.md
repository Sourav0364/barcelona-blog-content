## Be specific about what you need

People looking for gluten-free pizza may have different needs. Some are choosing by preference; others need to avoid gluten for health reasons. If avoiding cross-contact is important, say so clearly when you contact a restaurant. A gluten-free crust, if listed, does not by itself establish that a whole pizza is prepared in a separate area or with separate tools.

Before you compare local results, note what information you need:

- Is a gluten-free crust currently on the menu?
- Are the sauce, toppings, and any additions suitable for your requirements?
- How is the order prepared, baked, cut, and packaged?
- Are separate utensils, surfaces, or equipment used, if that matters to you?
- Can the restaurant explain its cross-contact procedures?

The restaurant’s current answer is more useful than assuming a menu tag settles every dietary question. If you have a serious allergy, follow your clinician’s advice and make your decision based on the restaurant’s direct explanation.

## Compare the complete order and service details

Once you find a potential option, check whether the entire order fits your needs—not just the crust name. Ask about current ingredients in the crust, sauce, cheese, toppings, and seasonings, and whether substitutions are possible. Recipes and suppliers can change, so a past review or photo may not describe the current menu.

If you plan to dine in, check current hours and the location. For pickup or delivery, ask whether the service is currently available for your address and how the restaurant handles packaging. Confirm the full current price and any fees before placing an order; do not assume a particular discount, bundle, or delivery area from a search snippet.

A useful comparison is based on information each restaurant confirms: current menu, preparation answers, order format, timing, location, and cost. If the answers are unclear, consider another option whose staff can address your questions directly.
