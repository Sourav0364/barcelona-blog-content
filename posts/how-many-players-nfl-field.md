**How many players are on an NFL field?** The 2026 [Official Playing Rules of the National Football League](https://static.www.nfl.com/image/upload/fl_attachment/league/tqivdkzt9mu6wdgsh1ku.pdf) set the game at **11 players per team**. When both teams line up with a full side, that is **22 players total**: 11 on offense and 11 on defense.

## The NFL’s 11-player rule, simply explained

Rule 5, Section 1, Article 1 of the 2026 NFL rulebook says a game is played by two teams of 11 players each. The rule also penalizes a team for having more than 11 players on the field when a snap, free kick, or fair-catch kick is made, subject to a narrow exception for a designated holder on certain kicks.

For a standard full lineup, the count is:

| Side of the play | Players |
|---|---:|
| Offense | 11 |
| Defense | 11 |
| Both teams combined | 22 |

The NFL rulebook defines the offense as the team in possession of the ball; its opponent is the defense. The teams switch those roles when possession changes. The count is about players participating on the field—not coaches, sideline personnel, substitutes, or game officials.

## Can an NFL team ever have a 12th person on the field?

There is a limited rulebook exception during a kickoff or safety kick: a uniformed designated holder may be the 12th person from the kicking team on the field to hold the ball. The rule identifies that holder as a **non-player** and says he must leave the field on his team’s sideline with the kicking tee immediately after the ball is kicked. This exception does not change the standard 11-player count for a team’s lineup.

## NFL player-count FAQ

### How many players from each NFL team are on the field?

The baseline is 11 players per team. With both full units on the field, there are 22 players total.

### Are there 11 offensive players and 11 defensive players?

Yes. One team is the offense while it has possession of the ball; the other team is the defense.

### Does the 12th-person exception mean an NFL team can play with 12 players?

No. The rulebook’s narrow holder exception is for a designated non-player holding the ball on a kickoff or safety kick; he must leave the field immediately after the kick.
