## What is the red zone in an NFL game?

The red zone is the stretch from the opponent’s 20-yard line toward the goal line. When a team is on offense and the ball is spotted at or inside that 20-yard area, game coverage may describe the offense as “in the red zone.” The [Merriam-Webster definition](https://www.merriam-webster.com/dictionary/red%20zone) uses the same opponent-20-yard-line reference.

A quick way to picture it is:

**Midfield → opponent’s 20-yard line → red zone → opponent’s goal line → end zone**

The direction of the attack matters. A team at its own 12-yard line is not in the opponent’s red zone; it has a long way to go. A team with the ball at the other side’s 12 is in the red zone, close to the goal it is attacking.

## Why the red zone matters when following a game

A red-zone trip is a promising field position, but it is not itself a scoring play. The offense still has to make progress on its downs. A drive that reaches this area can end in a touchdown, a field goal attempt, a turnover, or a change of possession without points. So “in the red zone” means the offense is close to the goal line—not that a touchdown has already happened.

As you follow an NFL drive, look at three things together:

1. **The yard line:** Is the ball near the opponent’s 20, or close to the goal line?
2. **Down and distance:** How many downs remain, and how many yards are needed to continue the drive?
3. **The result of the possession:** Did the offense finish with a touchdown, settle for a field goal, or lose possession?

You may also see a “red-zone touchdown rate” in game analysis. It describes how often a team turns chances from this field area into touchdowns; it is a statistic about a set of possessions, not a guarantee about the next play. When comparing published numbers, check the source’s definition and time period.

## Red zone is field position, not a special rule or viewing service

There is no extra score just for reaching the red zone. The phrase is shorthand for where the ball is on the field; normal game situations such as down, distance, and possession still matter. An offense can reach the 20 and then move closer, score, or see the other team take over.

## NFL red zone FAQ

### What yard line does the NFL red zone start at?

The red zone begins at the opponent’s 20-yard line and continues toward the goal line. It is a field-position term, not a separate rule.

### Does reaching the red zone mean a team will score?

No. The offense is closer to the goal line, but the drive can still end in a touchdown, a field goal attempt, a turnover, or a change of possession without points.

### Is a team in the red zone at its own 20-yard line?

No. “Red zone” refers to the area near the goal the offense is attacking. A team at its own 20 is far from its opponent’s goal line.
