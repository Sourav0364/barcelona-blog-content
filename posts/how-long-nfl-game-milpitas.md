The short answer to **“How long is an NFL game?”** has two parts: regulation has **60 minutes of game-clock time**, while the real-time viewing window is usually a little over three hours. The clock and the time between the scheduled kickoff and the final play are not the same measurement.

## The official NFL game clock: 60 minutes

The [NFL’s 2026 Rulebook, Rule 4](https://operations.nfl.com/rules-officiating/2026-nfl-rulebook), defines regulation length as **60 minutes divided into four periods of 15 minutes each**. That is the amount of playing time shown on the game clock across the four quarters; it is not a promise that the whole event will finish 60 minutes after kickoff.

The rulebook also sets intervals between periods and a halftime break. In the 2026 rules, halftime is **13 minutes**, and the first-to-second and third-to-fourth-period intermissions are at least two minutes. If the score is tied when regulation ends, the game can continue into overtime under Rule 16.

## Why the real-time NFL viewing window is longer

The game clock does not simply count down without interruption. The NFL rulebook specifies clock stoppages for situations including an incomplete forward pass, a runner going out of bounds, a change of possession, the two-minute warning, and an official timeout. Charged team timeouts and halftime also interrupt the playing time. Broadcast breaks add time beyond the game clock as well.

A useful planning estimate is **a little over three hours for the live NFL game**, but that is not a guaranteed end time. The game situation and stoppages vary, and overtime can extend the event beyond regulation. Lineups’ [updated 2026 NFL guide](https://www.lineups.com/articles/nfl-101-beginners-guide-how-nfl-works/) gives a similar real-time estimate; the NFL rulebook remains the authority on the clock itself.

## Scheduled kickoff is not a guaranteed finish time

A kickoff listing tells you when the game is scheduled to start; it does not supply an exact end time. If you are coordinating a group, catching the end of a game, or arranging a ride, leave room after the listed start instead of calculating the finish as exactly 60 minutes later.

For a live game, follow the current score and game clock through the official NFL or team game center. That tells you where play stands, but a close finish, stoppages, or overtime can still change when the broadcast ends. This guide is about timing in general, not a forecast for any specific NFL matchup.

## FAQ: NFL game length

### Is an NFL game only one hour long?

The official regulation game clock totals 60 minutes, split into four 15-minute periods. The full viewing window is longer because the clock stops and the event includes breaks; a game tied at the end of regulation can also continue into overtime.

### How long should I set aside to watch an NFL game?

A little over three hours is a useful general planning estimate, not a promised duration. Stoppages and the way a game unfolds affect the finish, and overtime can extend it. Check the current game listing for the scheduled kickoff and leave a buffer if you have a fixed departure time.

### How long is NFL halftime?

The 2026 NFL Rulebook sets halftime at 13 minutes. The total time away from the broadcast may feel different because the broadcast includes its own programming; check the event’s current listing for timing details.

### Can a game end exactly 60 minutes after kickoff?

Not necessarily. Sixty minutes is the total regulation game-clock time, not the elapsed duration from scheduled kickoff to the final whistle. The game clock stops during specified situations, and breaks and possible overtime add time.
