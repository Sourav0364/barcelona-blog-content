## Start with the meeting, not the search result

Before comparing Milpitas restaurants, note the basics of the dinner:

- **Purpose:** Is this an introductory client dinner, a team meal, or a working conversation?
- **Group:** How many people are expected, and does anyone have dietary or ingredient questions?
- **Setting:** Does the group need a quieter conversation, a shared-table feel, or something else? Ask the restaurant rather than guessing from a photo.
- **Food and drinks:** Are guests looking for shared plates, individual orders, craft cocktails, or a mix? Confirm specific current options directly.
- **Timing:** What date and time works for everyone, and is the restaurant serving then? Do not assume business-dinner hours from a general listing.

A search phrase helps create a shortlist; it cannot verify current hours, a menu, seating, or a restaurant’s ability to accommodate your group.

## Compare business-dinner details before inviting guests

When a dinner involves clients or colleagues, small logistics can affect the plan. Use this checklist for any restaurant you are considering:

1. **Confirm the exact address.** Send the full street address, not only a neighborhood or landmark.
2. **Ask about the setting.** If conversation is important, ask about the available space, expected sound, and seating for your party at the time you plan to arrive.
3. **Check the current menu.** Describe any dietary needs or ingredient questions, and ask what is currently available rather than relying on an old photo or post.
4. **Clarify the group arrangement.** Ask whether the restaurant can accommodate your party size and whether you need to make arrangements in advance; do not assume a reservation or private room is offered.
5. **Set expectations about the bill.** Ask about current prices and payment arrangements directly if they matter to your group; this guide does not state prices or policies.
6. **Confirm the timing.** Verify operating hours for the date you have in mind before sending a calendar invitation.

These checks make a “best restaurant” search more practical without turning a search result into a promise about a particular venue.
