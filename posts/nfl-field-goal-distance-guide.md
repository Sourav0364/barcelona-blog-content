When an NFL broadcast calls a **47-yard field goal attempt**, the number is not simply the distance from the line of scrimmage to the goal line. **NFL field goal distance is measured from the spot of the kick to the goalposts.** That includes the space between the line of scrimmage and the holder, plus the depth of the end zone.

## How the distance is measured

The kick’s distance runs from where the holder places the ball to the goalposts. The [NFL field layout](https://operations.nfl.com/rules-officiating/2026-nfl-rulebook) puts the goalposts at the back of the end zone; a recent [field-goal distance explainer](https://lindyssports.com/nfl/longest-field-goal-nfl-history) notes that the holder is typically about seven to eight yards behind the line of scrimmage and the end zone is ten yards deep.

So, the yard-line number alone does not tell you the listed field-goal distance. A useful estimate is:

**Distance ≈ yards from the line of scrimmage to the goal line + 10 yards for the end zone + about 7–8 yards to the holder.**

This is a practical estimate, not a fixed setup rule: the holder’s exact position can vary slightly.

## Example: a kick from the opponent’s 30-yard line

Suppose the ball is snapped from the opponent’s 30. The line of scrimmage is 30 yards from the goal line. Add the ten-yard end zone and roughly seven or eight yards from the line to the holder, and the attempt is about **47–48 yards**.

The same math explains why a kick from the opponent’s 20-yard line is roughly **37–38 yards**, not 20. The broadcast distance describes the kick’s path to the goalposts, not just how far the offense advanced downfield.

## Why this distinction helps fans

The measurement makes long attempts easier to follow on a field diagram or box score. It also gives context to a fourth-down decision: a listed distance is a useful description of the attempt, but it does not by itself predict whether the kick will succeed. The kicker, conditions, and game situation still matter.

The NFL Football Operations glossary defines a field goal as a place kick from the field of play over the crossbar and between the uprights, worth **three points**. Knowing how the yardage is counted helps separate the scoring result from the spot where the ball was snapped.

## NFL field-goal distance FAQ

### Is a field-goal distance measured from the line of scrimmage?

No. The listed distance is measured from the spot of the kick to the goalposts. The holder is behind the line of scrimmage, and the end zone adds ten yards beyond the goal line.

### Why is a field goal about 17 or 18 yards longer than the yard-line number?

The end zone is ten yards deep, and the holder commonly sets up about seven to eight yards behind the line of scrimmage. Together, those spaces add roughly 17–18 yards to the distance from the line to the goalposts.

### How many points is an NFL field goal worth?

A successful field goal is worth three points, according to the NFL Football Operations glossary.

## The takeaway

To estimate NFL field goal distance, start with the offense’s yards to the goal line, then add the ten-yard end zone and the holder’s position behind the line. That is why the attempt shown on the broadcast is longer than the yard-line number alone.
