## Ask what is available at lunchtime

A business listing may cover a restaurant generally without confirming what it serves or when lunch is available. Before making a short break depend on a particular place, ask:

- Is the restaurant currently serving lunch on the day and at the time I plan to visit?
- What food is available then, and can I review the current menu?
- What is the expected wait or preparation time for my visit?
- Is dine-in or pickup currently available for the option I need? Confirm rather than assuming.
- Can the restaurant answer any ingredient or dietary questions before I arrive?
- Are the current prices and payment details clear?

These questions help distinguish a genuinely workable plan from a result that only looks convenient in a directory. If timing matters, contact the restaurant directly on the day rather than relying on an old review or listing.

## Compare the whole break, not just distance

The nearest map pin is not always the best fit for a limited lunch window. Compare a few practical details:

1. **Route:** Check the journey from your starting point and back, not only the restaurant’s city name.
2. **Current service:** Confirm that lunch is being served at your intended time.
3. **Order fit:** Ask what is available and whether the format works for the amount of time you have.
4. **Dietary needs:** Verify ingredients and preparation directly if they matter to you.
5. **Contingency:** Have another nearby option in mind in case the wait or availability changes.

A query such as **“quick lunch in Milpitas”** describes what the searcher needs; it is not proof that a particular business can serve food quickly. Confirm the details instead of inferring speed from a rating, photo, or category.
