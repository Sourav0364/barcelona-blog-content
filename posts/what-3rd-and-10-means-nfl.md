**What does 3rd-and-10 mean in the NFL?** The offense is on its third down and needs 10 yards to reach the line to gain for a new first down. It is a short way to show both the current try and the distance still required. Once you know those two pieces, an NFL broadcast’s down-and-distance display becomes much easier to follow.

## What “3rd-and-10” tells you

The first number is the **down**: the offense’s first, second, third, or fourth attempt in the current series. The second number is the **distance** the offense needs to reach the line to gain. So “3rd-and-10” means third down with ten yards to go to earn a first down.

The NFL’s [Football Operations glossary](https://operations.nfl.com/rules-officiating/nfl-football-basics/football-terms) defines a down as a period of action that begins when the ball is put in play and ends when it is declared dead. It explains that an offense has four downs or fewer to advance the yards required for a first down. The [2026 NFL Rulebook](https://operations.nfl.com/rules-officiating/2026-nfl-rulebook) is the official place to check the full rules.

## How the NFL line to gain works

A down-and-distance display is a compact guide to the offense’s progress toward a new series:

- **1st-and-10:** first down, with ten yards to the line to gain in a typical situation.
- **2nd-and-4:** second down, with four yards still needed.
- **3rd-and-1:** third down, with one yard remaining to reach the line to gain.
- **4th-and-Goal:** fourth down, with the goal line as the target in a goal-to-go situation.

The target is normally ten yards beyond the spot where the series begins. When the goal line is closer than that, the situation can be shown as **“and Goal”** instead of a yardage number. The NFL glossary also describes the chain crew’s 10-yard chain as a tool to help officials track the first-down target.

## A simple example of downs and distance

Imagine a generic NFL drive that starts **1st-and-10**. The offense gains six yards, so the next display is **2nd-and-4**. A three-yard gain leaves **3rd-and-1**. The offense can earn a new first down by reaching the line to gain on any of its four downs; it does not need one single 10-yard play.

This is an arithmetic illustration, not a summary of an actual NFL game. If the offense reaches the target, the series resets. If it is still short after third down, it faces fourth down. On fourth down, a team might run or pass to try to gain the remaining yards, punt, or attempt a field goal; the appropriate choice depends on the situation and cannot be inferred from the down-and-distance display alone.

## Why the display matters while following a game

Down and distance helps explain what the offense needs on the next play. **3rd-and-1** and **3rd-and-10** are both third down, but the distance to the target is very different. The display does not tell you which play will be called or whether the offense will convert; it simply describes the down and the remaining distance.

## NFL down-and-distance FAQ

### What does 1st-and-10 mean?

It means the offense is on first down and, in a typical situation, needs ten yards to reach the line to gain for another first down.

### What does 3rd-and-1 mean?

It means third down with one yard remaining to reach the line to gain. The number after “and” is the distance, not the number of the next down.

### Does an offense have to gain ten yards on one play?

No. It can gain the required yards across its series of downs. Reaching the line to gain gives the offense a new first down and another series.

### Does 3rd-and-10 mean a team will go for it on fourth down?

No. The display shows the current down and distance, not the coach’s next decision. Fourth-down choices depend on the game situation.

## The takeaway

**3rd-and-10** means third down with ten yards needed to reach the line to gain. Read the first number as the down and the second as the remaining distance, then use the official NFL rulebook for rule-specific questions.
