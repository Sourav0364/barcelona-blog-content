Looking at an NFL box score and wondering what **passer rating** means? The number summarizes four parts of a quarterback’s passing line: completion rate, yards per attempt, touchdown-pass rate, and interception rate. Understanding those inputs makes the statistic easier to read without treating one number as a full evaluation of a player or a game.

## What NFL passer rating measures

The familiar NFL passer rating is calculated from four rates, rather than from a single total-yard figure:

1. **Completion rate:** completions divided by pass attempts.
2. **Yards per attempt:** passing yards divided by pass attempts.
3. **Touchdown-pass rate:** touchdown passes divided by attempts.
4. **Interception rate:** interceptions divided by attempts.

The [ESPN NFL statistics glossary](https://www.espn.com/nfl/statistics/glossary.html) lists these passing-stat terms and lays out the passer-rating calculation. Each component is converted to a value capped between 0 and 2.375; the four values are added, divided by six, and multiplied by 100. That puts the NFL formula’s maximum at **158.3**.

In practical terms, the formula rewards completion efficiency, yards gained per attempt, and touchdown passes, while interceptions reduce the rating. It is a summary of those passing inputs—not a count of every useful play in a game.

## How to read it beside the box score

If you are searching **how to read NFL passing stats**, keep the rating next to the line that produced it. Check completions and attempts, then look at passing yards, touchdowns, and interceptions. The NFL statistics glossary also defines separate fields for sacks and sack yards, while rushing statistics have their own categories.

That distinction matters: the standard passer-rating calculation uses completions, attempts, passing yards, passing touchdowns, and interceptions. It does not directly include a quarterback’s rushing production or sacks. A passer rating therefore is not a complete measure of a quarterback’s total contribution, and it cannot tell you the game result on its own. Read the surrounding box score and game context as well.

The NFL’s [player statistics page](https://www.nfl.com/stats/player-stats/) is a current place to check NFL player statistics. This guide explains the metric; it does not analyze a particular player, team, score, or upcoming game.

## Why the number is useful—and where to be careful

Passer rating offers a compact way to compare the four passing-rate inputs in a box score. It can help fans spot the difference between a line built on efficient completions and one affected by interceptions, but it should not replace the underlying numbers.

A quick game-stat checklist:

- **Volume:** How many attempts and completions are listed?
- **Efficiency:** What are the completion rate and yards per attempt?
- **Scoring:** How many passing touchdowns appear in the line?
- **Mistakes:** What is the interception count?
- **Context:** What do the separate rushing, sack, and team statistics add to the picture?

No single statistic captures every aspect of an NFL game. The formula tells you about selected passing outcomes; the rest of the box score helps explain the broader game.

## NFL passer rating FAQ

### What stats go into NFL passer rating?

Completion rate, yards per attempt, touchdown-pass rate, and interception rate. The formula applies limits to each component before combining them.

### What is the maximum NFL passer rating?

The formula’s maximum is **158.3**. A rating should still be read alongside the passing line and the rest of the game statistics.

### Does NFL passer rating include rushing yards or sacks?

No. The passer-rating formula uses passing completions, attempts, yards, touchdowns, and interceptions. Rushing and sack statistics are separate parts of the box score.

### Does a high passer rating tell me which team won?

No. It describes selected passing statistics for a quarterback; it is not the final score or a complete summary of an NFL game.

## The takeaway

**NFL passer rating explained** in one line: it combines four passing-rate components—completions, yards per attempt, touchdown passes, and interceptions—into a score capped at 158.3. Use it as one box-score clue, then look at the rest of the NFL game statistics for context.
