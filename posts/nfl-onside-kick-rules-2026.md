## What changed in the NFL onside kick rule for 2026?

The NFL’s official [2026 rulebook](https://operations.nfl.com/rules-officiating/2026-nfl-rulebook) lists Rule 6-1-6 as permitting the kicking team to declare an onside kick at any time during the game. The NFL’s [announcement of approved 2026 playing-rule changes](https://www.nfl.com/news/nfl-announces-new-playing-rules-bylaws-resolutions-for-2026) also includes that change.

The change concerns **when the kicking team can declare an onside kick**. In prior seasons, the rules changed in stages: teams first could declare one only while trailing in the fourth quarter; last season, a trailing team could declare one at any point in the game. For 2026, the requirement that the kicking team be behind was removed. ESPN’s [2026 rules explainer](https://www.espn.com/nfl/story/_/id/49868175/nfl-rules-changes-2026-red-challenge-flags-kickoffs-penalties-hip-drop) describes the new option as available whether a team is ahead, tied, or behind.

This is a rule change—not a prediction about a particular game, team, or strategy. The NFL rulebook is the authority for the full procedure and any conditions on a play.

## What is an onside kick in an NFL game?

An onside kick is a kickoff option intended to give the kicking team a chance to regain possession rather than simply send the ball deep for the receiving team. The rulebook describes the attempt as something the kicking team must **declare**. That distinction matters: a regular kickoff and a declared onside-kick attempt are not interchangeable terms.

The 2026 change expands the circumstances in which the kicking team may choose the declared attempt. It does not turn every kickoff into an onside kick, and it does not guarantee the kicking team will recover the ball. The receiving team can still recover it, and the outcome depends on the play. For exact details about positioning, eligibility, penalties, or how a ruling is applied, consult the current NFL rulebook rather than relying on a short summary.

## Can the team that is ahead attempt an onside kick?

Yes. The 2026 rule allows the kicking team to declare an onside kick at any point during the game, and ESPN’s current-season explanation says that includes when the team is ahead, tied, or behind. That is the most important answer for fans who remember the previous trailing-team restriction.

“Can attempt” is not the same as “will attempt.” A rule change does not tell viewers which option a coach will choose in a specific game. The score, clock, field position, game situation, and team decision all belong to the live game; do not infer a planned onside kick from a score alone.

## Is the onside kick still declared?

The 2026 rule is worded as permission for the kicking team to **declare** an onside kick. CBS Sports’ [2026 season primer](https://www.cbssports.com/nfl/news/2026-nfl-season-primer-what-to-know-entering-week-1/) notes that teams still cannot attempt a surprise onside kick or stack one side of the field. The practical point for a viewer is to watch for the announced choice and the officials’ formation and signal rather than assuming a standard kickoff is an onside attempt.

For any close call, penalty, or question about the legal setup, the full 2026 NFL rulebook takes precedence over this overview or an informal explanation during a broadcast.

## Does the new rule guarantee a recovery?

No. The change expands when a team is allowed to declare an onside kick; it does not guarantee the kicking team will recover the ball or keep possession. It also does not establish that teams will attempt one more often, or that the choice will be effective in a particular situation.

A concise way to remember the rule is: **the option is available at any point in the game, for a team ahead, tied, or behind; recovery is a separate outcome.** Fans looking for a recap or live explanation should distinguish the rule’s permission from what happened on the field in that specific game.

The 2025 change allowed a trailing team to declare one at any point in the game. The 2026 change removes the trailing requirement altogether.
